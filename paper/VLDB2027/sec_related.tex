\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{AI-augmented data systems.}
Palimpzest, DocETL, LOTUS, Abacus, and QUEST expose semantic operators or
document-processing pipelines and optimize models, prompts, operator
order, and access paths~\cite{liu2025palimpzest,shankar2024docetl,
patel2024lotus,russo2025abacus,chai2025quest}. KEN develops adaptive model
cascades in an execution engine for unstructured DBMSs
\cite{kossmann2026ken}; LEGO-GraphRAG decomposes GraphRAG into a modular
design space~\cite{cao2025lego}. This literature motivates treating
retrieval and generation as physical operators. \system addresses a
different optimizer decision: whether the selected policy satisfies a
service-level risk constraint after plan search.

LOTUS provides accuracy guarantees for implementations of individual
semantic operators~\cite{patel2024lotus}. Semantic integrity constraints
propose declarative correctness conditions and proactive or reactive
enforcement for AI-augmented pipelines~\cite{lee2025sic}. Evidence
contracts are complementary in scope: they bound population violation
rates for an end-to-end adaptive policy, and connect those constraints to
physical-plan selection and online recertification.

\textbf{Adaptive RAG and routing.}
Adaptive-RAG, Self-RAG, corrective retrieval, and model routers change
retrieval or model strength per query
\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag,asai2024selfrag,yan2024corrective,
chen2023frugalgpt,ong2024routellm,ding2024hybridllm}. They provide useful
candidate policies for \system, but tune their dispatch rules empirically.
Conformal cascades and calibrated early exits control fixed model or
cascade decisions~\cite{fisch2021admission,schuster2022calm}. Our policy
space instead combines heterogeneous sources, retrieval plans, reranking,
verification, and generators under one end-to-end contract.

\textbf{Distribution-free risk control.}
TRAQ and C-RAG certify fixed RAG pipelines
\cite{li2024traq,kang2024crag}; conformal language generation and
factuality methods calibrate fixed decoding or abstention rules
\cite{quach2024conformallm,mohri2024factuality}. \system uses
Learn-then-Test and Hoeffding--Bentkus tests
\cite{angelopoulos2021ltt,bentkus2004hoeffding}, but the tested object is a
policy selected from a physical-plan space and the objective is execution
cost.

\textbf{Query optimization and online inference.}
The plan lattice, access-path rule, and progressive execution follow the
database lineage of cost-based optimization, Cascades, and mid-query
reoptimization~\cite{selinger1979access,graefe1995cascades,
kabra1998efficient,markl2004robust}. The difference is semantic:
non-equivalent plans require statistical feasibility rather than an
algebraic equivalence proof. After deployment, e-processes and e-detectors
provide time-uniform false-alarm control
\cite{ramdas2023savi,shin2024edetectors}. We use them to connect monitored
contract violations to the optimizer's recertification loop.
