\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

We study four questions: (1) Does optimization preserve the declared
contract across calibration samples? (2) How much does certification cost?
(3) Do vouchers, group contracts, and monitoring behave as designed?
(4) Does the system generalize across physical-plan choices and scale to
large policy spaces?

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\textbf{Workloads.}
\emph{HybridQA} joins Wikipedia tables with linked passages
\cite{chen2020hybridqa}; loss is
$\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{tokenF1}<0.5]$ and the train/calibration/test sizes are
5000/3000/3466.
\emph{CRAG} combines web retrieval with the benchmark's structured API
\cite{yang2024cragbench}; correctness and hallucination follow its official
grader, evidence has crawl timestamps, and splits are 700/1000/1006.
\emph{ASQA} and \emph{QAMPARI} use the ALCE protocol
\cite{stelmakh2022asqa,amouyal2022qampari,gao2023alce} and the official
TRUE-NLI citation evaluator~\cite{honovich2022true}. Citation loss is
$\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{citationRecall}<50]$; their splits are 150/350/448 and
150/350/500. The tracks use every query in the corresponding public
release.

Each workload has four cost-ascending rungs, from sparse retrieval with a
small generator to hybrid retrieval, reranking, verification, optional
structured-API evidence, repair, and the strongest generator. All model
calls are real and token metered. Escalation pays the cumulative cost of
visited rungs; a router pays the selected rung.

\textbf{Baselines.} Fixed plans execute one rung for the whole workload.
The complexity router adapts Adaptive-RAG~\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}; the
utility router maximizes predicted sufficiency minus weighted cost.
BO-tuned uses Optuna TPE~\cite{akiba2019optuna} over escalation thresholds.
Abacus~\cite{russo2025abacus} uses its constrained, point-estimate plan
selection with a 150-execution budget. Empirical-no-cert uses \system's
candidate space but selects the cheapest policy whose empirical
calibration risk is at most $\alpha$. Every method reads the same execution
matrix.

\textbf{Repeated-draw protocol.}
For each track, we pool the calibration and test execution records into a
fixed population. In each of 1000 trials, methods select a policy from a
random calibration subset, and we measure that policy's exact risk over the
whole population. The reported failure probability is therefore
$\Pr(R(\hat\pi)>\alpha)$, exactly the quantity bounded by $\delta$.
Sampling is without replacement. For a population of $N$ binary losses
with $K_0=\lfloor N\alpha\rfloor+1$, the null tail
$p_{\mathrm{hyp}}(k)=\Pr[\mathrm{Hyp}(N,K_0,n)\leq k]$ is exact. We
exhaustively verified for every experimental $(N,n,\alpha)$ that the
Hoeffding--Bentkus rejection region used by \system is contained in this
exact rejection region, and reran the headline trials with
$\min(p_{\mathrm{KL}},p_{\mathrm{hyp}})$. Both tests satisfy the contract;
the exact test certifies deeper prefixes. The checks and trial-level
outputs are in the artifact.

At matched contracts, \system reduces cost relative to the strongest plan
by $6.9\times$ on HybridQA, $20\times$ on CRAG, $1.4\times$ on ASQA, and
$15\times$ on QAMPARI (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Methods that are cheaper on
HybridQA or ASQA exceed the selected contract. On QAMPARI, certification
selects the same low-cost routing policy as the utility router; the
difference is that the former has passed a valid feasibility test. The
matched oracle knows every query outcome in advance and lower-bounds the
same constrained objective. \system is within $1.4$--$6.7\times$ of this
unattainable bound.

Adding a final deferral action extends the contract with a bound on human
review volume. At answer-risk $\alpha=.1$, \system certifies HybridQA and
ASQA with a maximum deferral rate $\beta=.75$; observed deferral is .597
and .219, respectively. At $\alpha=.2$, all four tracks certify. The
optimizer therefore exposes strictness as an explicit tradeoff between
automated risk, cost, and review load.

\subsection{Optimizer and runtime components}
\label{sec:exp-components}

\textbf{Rewrite vouchers.}
On 1000 HybridQA queries, four rewrites reduce a reference plan from 1.74
to 0.21 mCNY/query. Across 1000 calibration redraws, individual 95\%
vouchers cover their exact harm rates in 98.9--99.5\% of trials. The
composed plan harms 24.0\% of queries. A base-relative composition is not
valid because 8\% of harmed queries arise only from rewrite interaction.
The path voucher of Proposition~\ref{prop:composition} covers every redraw;
its bound is .376, compared with .251 for direct certification. This
supports the intended division: vouchers order the lattice, and the final
policy is certified directly. Selectivity conditioning further separates a
.075 pushdown harm rate on vague predicates from no observed harm at
$\sigma=1$.

\textbf{Group contracts.}
On CRAG, a marginal policy exceeds the real-time group's target in 99\% of
200 draws. Group-conditional certification reduces every group's
deployment-failure rate to at most 3.5\% by assigning more KG-API
executions to the 9\% real-time slice. It also identifies groups for which
no physical plan meets the requested limit.

\textbf{Drift and recertification.}
A 3000-query CRAG stream changes from static/slow to fast/real-time at
$t=1500$. The static policy's violation rate rises from .58 to .88 against
$\alpha=.66$. The monitor alarms after a median of 78 queries (36--125
over 20 seeds), with no false alarm in 200 drift-free streams, and
localizes the changepoint within a median of four queries for the strongest
shift. A 250-query post-alarm window then certifies a new policy. Across ten
streams, the loop yields phase-two risk .819 versus .886 without
recertification and .815 for an oracle that knows the change time.

Auditing all CRAG queries costs .29 mCNY/query, bringing deployment plus
monitoring cost to .60 mCNY/query and retaining a $10\times$ saving over
the strongest plan. Independent 25\% and 10\% audit rates increase median
delay to 343 and 878 wall-clock queries while increasing savings to
$15.8\times$ and $17.9\times$.

\textbf{Multiple constraints.}
On CRAG, one policy jointly certifies correctness $\leq.65$,
hallucination $\leq.30$, staleness $\leq.05$, and 8-second latency
exceedance $\leq.10$. Its test risks are .603, .258, $<.003$, and .057 at
5.02 mCNY/query. The intersection--union test certifies 47 candidates,
versus 44 after splitting $\delta$ across constraints. On ASQA, the same
comparison is decisive: the intersection--union test certifies 45
candidates for a four-constraint contract, while Bonferroni certifies
none.

\subsection{Physical planning, portability, and scale}
\label{sec:exp-system}

On HybridQA, we compare table-first and text-first access paths with
lexical or hybrid retrieval at a fixed generator. Pool selectivity predicts
a consistent crossover. At $\alpha=.5$, the certified mixed access-path
policy has test risk .481 and costs .337 mCNY/query, 31\% less than the
strongest fixed path (.492 mCNY/query). Thus cardinality estimates improve
cost while the statistical test supplies feasibility.

Replacing only the generator column on shared retrieval results confirms
that the optimizer is not tied to one price schedule. Certified savings
relative to each family's strongest plan are $1.1\times$ for Qwen,
$16.6\times$ for DeepSeek, $5.6\times$ for GLM, and $1.2\times$ for
locally served Gemma~3. In 200 repeated draws per family, no certified
policy exceeds its contract; empirical selection reaches 30\%. When the
Gemma plan space cannot meet $\alpha=.35$, \system reports no feasible
candidate.

Finally, expanding the threshold grid from 69 to 97,365 candidates takes
8.5\,s on HybridQA, 4.1\,s on CRAG, and .9\,s on ASQA. Materializing
retrieval once across four generator families removes three quarters of
duplicated retrieval work. These results confirm that certification is a
small vectorized pass over execution records, rather than an additional
model-serving workload.
