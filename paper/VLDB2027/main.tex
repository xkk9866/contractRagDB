\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented data services execute queries over tables, text
collections, vector indexes, and structured APIs. A query can therefore
admit many physical plans with very different costs, but these plans are
not semantically equivalent: cheaper retrieval, reranking, and generation
choices may change the answer. Existing optimizers select plans from
estimated quality and cost, so their selected plan has no finite-sample
guarantee of meeting an application's quality, citation, freshness, or
latency target. We present \emph{evidence contracts}, a declarative
interface that bounds service-level violation rates, and \system, a query
optimizer that minimizes cost subject to such contracts. \system generates
a plan lattice with cost-reducing rewrites, orders it using
selectivity-aware risk vouchers, and constructs fixed, routed, and
progressive-execution policies. A fixed-sequence statistical test then
certifies the deployed policy without a multiplicity penalty from plan
search. An anytime-valid monitor detects post-deployment drift and invokes
recertification. Experiments on HybridQA, CRAG, ASQA, and QAMPARI use
token-metered executions and exact finite-population risk over repeated
calibration draws. \system is the only tested optimizer that stays within
the prescribed $\delta=0.1$ failure budget at every contract level; its
worst observed deployment-failure rate is 0.8\%, whereas point-estimate
optimizers reach 64\%. At matched contracts, \system reduces cost by up to
$20\times$ relative to the strongest fixed plan and certifies plan spaces
containing nearly $10^5$ policies in seconds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Retrieval-augmented data systems need an optimizer whose feasibility
decision remains valid when physical plans can change the answer.
Evidence contracts make the acceptable service-level risk explicit.
\system separates search from certification: learned scores, cardinality
estimates, and rewrite vouchers determine which policies are explored,
whereas a fixed-sequence test alone authorizes deployment. Progressive
execution and anytime-valid monitoring extend the same contract to
runtime. The result is a practical path from point-estimate plan selection
to risk-certified query optimization over heterogeneous evidence sources.
