\begin{abstract}
Dynamic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) learns when a language
model should retrieve, rerank, or escalate to a stronger generator.
These policies can improve the accuracy--cost trade-off, but their
scores do not bound population risk and may become unreliable after
knowledge drift. We introduce \system, a learning-and-certification
method for progressive RAG inference. Candidate policies use learned
sufficiency scores over retrieval and generation signals to stop at the
cheapest adequate rung. Risk vouchers summarize the observed effect of
plan rewrites and order a large policy family. Soundness comes from a
separate distribution-free fixed-sequence test: with probability at
least $1-\delta$, every loss in the selected evidence contract meets
its target, regardless of candidate count or score quality. We also
prove a cost-consistency result and give a restart-mixture e-process
whose drift-detection delay is independent of the change time. A
geometric schedule preserves validity through repeated
recertification. Experiments on HybridQA, CRAG, ASQA, and QAMPARI cover
four model families, two serving backends, and ablations of scoring,
policy families, calibration size, candidate density, audit rate, and
drift. Across 1000 calibration draws, selected policies violate their
contracts in at most 0.8\% of draws under a 10\% error budget, whereas
empirical and Bayesian selection reach 64\%. The policies reduce model
cost by up to 20$\times$ (10--18$\times$ with audits). The method is
orthogonal to the underlying retriever or reranker and provides a
general certification layer for learned adaptive RAG.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This work develops a certification layer for learned progressive RAG
policies. Sufficiency scores adapt computation to each query, and risk
vouchers organize candidate policies, but neither is trusted as a
certificate. A distribution-free fixed-sequence test controls the
selected policy's loss even when those learned components are
miscalibrated. The cost-consistency result explains when a well-ordered
family recovers the least-cost feasible policy. The restart-mixture
e-process then extends the method to non-stationary knowledge sources
with a delay bound independent of when the change occurs.

Experiments on four public benchmarks and four model families verify
the certificate against exact population risk and probe the
learning and selection mechanisms through score, family,
calibration-size, dense-grid, audit-rate, and drift studies. Contract
violations occurred in at most 0.8\% of 1000 draws under a 10\% budget,
compared with up to 64\% for uncertified selection. Cost fell by up to
20$\times$, or 10--18$\times$ after audits. ContractRAG supplies a
reusable certification layer for progressive policies constructed from
retrieval-learning methods. This modular design keeps the guarantee
reusable as retrievers,
rerankers, and generators improve.
