\textit{Neurocomputing}

\textbf{Re: Research article submission, ``Distribution-free
certification of learned progressive policies for retrieval-augmented
generation under knowledge drift''}

Dear Editor,

On behalf of all authors, we submit this manuscript for consideration
as a Research Article in \textit{Neurocomputing}.

Dynamic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) learns when to retrieve,
rerank, stop, or escalate to a stronger model. Such mechanisms improve
average accuracy and efficiency, but the learned score itself does not
bound the population risk of the policy selected for deployment. This
problem becomes more serious when many policy thresholds are searched
or the external knowledge distribution changes.

Our manuscript introduces ContractRAG, a learning-and-certification
method for progressive RAG inference. Per-rung sufficiency models use
retrieval and generation signals to control adaptive evidence
acquisition. Composable risk vouchers order policies generated from a
rewrite lattice. A separate distribution-free fixed-sequence algorithm
then certifies the selected policy independently of candidate count and
score quality. We prove finite-sample contract validity and cost
consistency. We also develop a restart-mixture e-process with
change-time-independent detection delay, constant-time updates, and a
lifetime guarantee across repeated recertification.

The evaluation uses HybridQA, CRAG, ASQA, and QAMPARI, four model
families, and two serving backends. It includes ablations of score
features and policy families, calibration-size scaling, dense candidate
grids, strict and joint contracts, audit rates, and controlled drift.
Across 1,000 calibration draws, selected policies exceed their
contracts in at most 0.8\% of draws under a 10\% error budget, whereas
empirical and Bayesian selection reach 64\%. Model cost falls by up to
20$\times$, or 10--18$\times$ after auditing.

The paper connects learned reranking, iterative retrieval, knowledge
refinement, and uncertainty-aware retrieval through a general
selection and certification layer that incorporates them as policies
or rungs. We believe this combination of learned adaptive computation, statistical
learning guarantees, drift adaptation, public-benchmark experiments,
and mechanism-focused ablations fits the journal's interest in learning
methods, computational learning theory, and resource-aware neural
systems.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is
not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the
manuscript and its submission. The authors declare no competing
financial interests or personal relationships that could have
influenced the work.

Thank you for considering our submission.
