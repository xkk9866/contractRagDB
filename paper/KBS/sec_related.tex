\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Distribution-free risk control}
Conformal prediction \citep{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021gentle}
and its risk-controlling extensions -- RCPS \citep{bates2021rcps},
Learn-then-Test \citep{angelopoulos2021ltt}, conformal risk control
\citep{angelopoulos2024conformalrisk} -- turn a calibration sample into
a finite-sample guarantee on a population risk, and are the statistical
foundation of certified RAG. Applications to retrieval pipelines include
TRAQ \citep{li2024traq}, C-RAG \citep{kang2024crag} and conformal
factuality \citep{mohri2024factuality}; recent work extends the machinery
to hallucination filtering \citep{cherian2024llmuncertainty}, and there
is work relaxing exchangeability itself by reweighting the calibration
sample \citep{barber2023beyond}. All of this yields
Definition~\ref{def:certified}: a bound on an unobservable population
mean, valid with probability $1-\delta$ under exchangeability or a
quantified departure from it.
Our contribution is orthogonal to how tight such a bound can be made. We
argue that for a rate contract over a served stream the population mean
is the wrong target, because the quantity an operator is held to is the
realised rate on the log, and that quantity can be controlled exactly.

\paragraph{Anytime-valid inference}
E-values, test martingales and confidence sequences
\citep{howard2021timeuniform,ramdas2023gametheoretic,waudbysmith2024betting}
give bounds that hold at all stopping times, and are the natural tool
for monitoring a deployed model; e-detectors \citep{shin2024edetectors}
extend them to changepoint detection, and our own earlier design used a
restart-mixture e-process to trigger recertification. These methods
weaken the fixed-sample assumption but keep the probabilistic form, and
they still require the observations to be independent of the past given
the model. The ledger needs neither: its guarantee is an accounting
identity, so an anytime bound is not something it achieves at a
confidence level but something it satisfies by construction. We use a
confidence sequence in this paper only to report diagnostics.

\paragraph{Selective prediction and abstention}
Rejecting inputs rather than predicting on them is classical
\citep{chow1970optimum,geifman2017selective,elyaniv2010foundations} and
has been revisited for language models, where conformal factuality
filters remove unsupported claims rather than whole answers
\citep{mohri2024factuality}. That literature asks a predictor to abstain to
raise its accuracy on what remains, and characterises the resulting
risk--coverage curve. We use abstention differently: not as a property of
one model but as an \emph{action} in an optimizer's space, priced
alongside the plans and scheduled by a cost objective. This is what makes
the risk--cost frontier continuous (Proposition~\ref{prop:feasible}) and
what makes contracts below the risk floor attainable. The connection to
selective prediction is that our decline rate at a strict contract is
exactly the coverage that literature would report; the difference is that
we choose it to minimise total cost, rather than sweeping it.

\paragraph{Online resource allocation and bandits with knapsacks}
Serving a stream under a consumable budget is the subject of bandits with
knapsacks \citep{badanidiyuru2018bwk,agrawal2014bwk} and online packing
\citep{devanur2019adwords}, where an optimistic LP over arms subject to
budget constraints achieves $O(\sqrt{T})$ regret. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ledger}
is recognisably in that family, and Theorem~\ref{thm:regret} adapts the
standard analysis. What is different is the role of the budget. In
knapsack problems the budget is a resource whose exhaustion ends the
episode, and the guarantee is about regret; here the budget \emph{is} the
contract, exhaustion means declining rather than stopping, and the
guarantee we care about is the pathwise feasibility that the gate
enforces regardless of what the LP does. To our knowledge the observation
that a quality contract can be enforced this way -- and therefore removed
from the statistical problem entirely -- has not been made.

\paragraph{Cost-aware LLM and RAG pipelines}
Cascades and routers reduce spend by sending easy queries to cheap models
\citep{chen2023frugalgpt,ding2024hybridllm,hu2024routerbench,
jeong2024adaptiverag}; workload-level optimizers such
as Abacus \citep{russo2025abacus} choose configurations under a quality
constraint using point estimates; systems work on RAG serving optimises
latency and throughput by caching intermediate state
\citep{jin2024ragcache}.
These give no feasibility statement, and our experiments confirm that
point-estimate selection violates its constraint on a large fraction of
draws. Conversely, the certified line of work above fixes the pipeline
and does not optimise cost. This paper's setting -- minimise cost subject
to a contract that must hold on the realised stream -- requires both, and
the ledger is what lets the two be designed separately.

\paragraph{Query optimization}
The separation we propose is standard in databases: cardinality estimates
choose plans, are wrong by orders of magnitude \citep{leis2015good}, and
correctness is the executor's responsibility rather than the estimator's;
adaptive and robust query processing revises decisions mid-execution
\citep{avnur2000eddies,babu2005proactive,markl2004robust}. Bringing that
discipline to RAG requires deciding what plays the role of the executor's
correctness invariant, and the answer here is the ledger. The plan-grid
experiment of Section~\ref{sec:res:grid} makes the analogy concrete in the
other direction too: under certification-by-inference, enlarging the
search space can degrade the chosen plan, which is a failure mode no
database optimizer would tolerate.
