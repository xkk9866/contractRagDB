\section{The \system\ framework}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Framework overview}
\label{sec:overview}

\begin{figure*}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/fig_overview.png}
\caption{Overview of the \system\ framework. An evidence contract
(stage 1) declares the reliability targets; a rewrite layer (stage 2)
generates a lattice of cost-decreasing plan variants annotated with
risk vouchers (Section~\ref{sec:rewrites}); a policy layer (stage 3)
lifts static plans into progressive knowledge-acquisition policies
(Section~\ref{sec:families}); a certification layer (stage 4) walks the
candidates in a train-data order and deploys the cheapest policy in
the certified prefix of a distribution-free fixed-sequence risk test
(Section~\ref{sec:certification}); the executor runs the certified
policy while an anytime-valid e-process monitor (stage 5) defends the
certificate against knowledge drift, re-entering certification on an
alarm or at the end of a monitoring horizon with geometrically
scheduled budgets (Section~\ref{sec:monitor}). Stages 2--3 use the
training split only; calibration data is touched exactly once, in
stage 4.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the architecture. \system\ consists of four
components. (i) A \emph{rewrite layer} generates a lattice of
cost-decreasing plan variants, each annotated with an empirical
\emph{risk voucher} that upper-bounds its probability of harming an
answer. (ii) A \emph{policy layer} lifts static plans into per-query
adaptive policies: escalation ladders, cost-utility routers, and
group-conditional variants. (iii) A \emph{certification layer} walks
the candidate policies in a data-independent order and outputs the
cheapest member of the prefix certified by a distribution-free risk
test. (iv) A
\emph{monitoring layer} watches deployed violations with an
anytime-valid e-detector and triggers certified recalibration on alarm.
Only the certification layer touches calibration data, and it does so
exactly once; the rewrite and policy layers use a disjoint training
split. Vouchers and learned scores guide the search order --- like a
cost model in a database optimizer, they may be wrong without
compromising correctness, only optimality. This division of labor is
the central design principle: \emph{machine learning decides how much
the certified policy saves; statistics alone decides whether the
contract holds.}

\subsection{Cost-decreasing rewrites and risk vouchers}
\label{sec:rewrites}

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_rewrites.pdf}
\caption{The rewrite lattice. Each edge is a cost-decreasing rewrite
annotated with a risk voucher; a maximal chain of rewrites yields the
cost-ascending ladder used by the policy layer.}
\label{fig:rewrites}
\end{figure}

A rewrite rule $R : P \mapsto P'$ transforms a plan into a cheaper one
(Figure~\ref{fig:rewrites}): retrieval truncation ($k \to k' < k$),
reranker elision, source pre-filter pushdown (restricting a passage
pool to rows selected by a structured predicate), generator downgrade
($g \to g'$ with a cheaper model), and repair elision. Classical query
optimizers require $P \equiv P'$; here equivalence fails, and we
quantify the damage.

\begin{definition}[Risk voucher]
\label{def:voucher}
For loss $\ell$ and rewrite $R$, let
$\rho(R; P) = \Prob_{q}\big(\ell(R(P), q) > \ell(P, q)\big)$ be the
\emph{harm probability}. An $(\hat\eps, \delta_r)$-\emph{voucher} for
$R$ at $P$ is a Clopper--Pearson upper bound $\hat\eps$ on $\rho(R;P)$
at confidence $1-\delta_r$, computed from training executions of $P$
and $R(P)$ on the same queries.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Assumption-free composition along paths}
The naive way to compose vouchers measures each rewrite against the
base plan and sums the bounds; that requires a no-synergistic-harm
(NSH) assumption --- that the chain harms no query unless some
individual rewrite already does --- which real workloads violate
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-rewrites}). \system\ instead vouchers the
\emph{path}: for the chain
$P_0 \xrightarrow{R_1} P_1 \xrightarrow{R_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{R_m}
P_m$, each step voucher $\hat\eps_i^{\mathrm{step}}$ bounds the harm of
$R_i$ against its actual predecessor $P_{i-1}$, not against $P_0$.

\begin{proposition}[Telescoping path composition]
\label{prop:composition}
If $\ell(P_m, q) > \ell(P_0, q)$ then some consecutive step strictly
increases the loss, so deterministically the event
$\{q : \ell(P_m,q) > \ell(P_0,q)\}$ is contained in
$\bigcup_{i} \{q : \ell(P_i,q) > \ell(P_{i-1},q)\}$.
Hence if each step carries an
$(\hat\eps_i^{\mathrm{step}}, \delta_r)$-voucher, then with probability
at least $1 - m\delta_r$,
$\rho(R_m \cdots R_1; P_0) \le \sum_{i=1}^m \hat\eps_i^{\mathrm{step}}$
--- with no behavioral assumption on rewrite interactions.
\end{proposition}

The inclusion is a tautology of the telescoping decomposition
$\ell(P_m) - \ell(P_0) = \sum_i [\ell(P_i) - \ell(P_{i-1})]$; the only
stochastic step is the $m$ Clopper--Pearson bounds
(proof in \ref{app:proofs}). The price is that step vouchers are
chain-specific, which matches how the optimizer explores anyway: it
descends one path of the rewrite lattice at a time.
\emph{Selectivity-conditioned vouchers} refine this further: the harm
of a pre-filter pushdown concentrates on queries whose structured
predicate is unselective (the restricted pool drops relevant
evidence), so \system\ stratifies calibration queries by estimated
pool selectivity $\sigma$ (Section~\ref{sec:planner}) and issues a
per-stratum Clopper--Pearson bound with a Bonferroni correction across
strata. The result is a voucher that is tight where the rewrite is
safe and honest where it is not. Even so, \system\ never uses vouchers
as deployment guarantees: they order the search space, while soundness
comes exclusively from direct certification of the deployed policy.

\subsection{Cardinality-driven knowledge-source selection}
\label{sec:planner}

Hybrid RAG queries \emph{join} a structured source with an unstructured
one: on HybridQA-style workloads, the evidence must connect table rows
(selected by a structured predicate) with linked passages. Two
acquisition orders exist. \emph{Table-first} pushes the predicate down
--- select rows, then retrieve only from the selected rows' passage
pool (small pool, cheap, but drops evidence linked from unselected
rows); \emph{text-first} retrieves over the whole table's pool and
joins back to rows (large pool, higher recall on vague predicates).
Which order wins is decided by the predicate's \emph{selectivity}.
\system\ estimates cardinalities before retrieval: the number of rows
matching the predicate, and the pushdown and full pool sizes from the
link table, giving a pool selectivity
$\sigma = |T_{\mathrm{push}}| / |T_{\mathrm{full}}|$ per query. A cost
model fit by least squares on measured executions and a recall model
$r(\sigma)$ fit on the training split drive a classical selection rule:
the cheapest acquisition path whose estimated recall clears the target;
otherwise the highest-recall path. As with vouchers, cardinality
estimates affect only cost, never the certificate. The same estimates
index the selectivity-conditioned vouchers of
Section~\ref{sec:rewrites}, so the planner and the risk layer share one
selectivity abstraction.

\paragraph{Operator sharing and materialization}
Retrieval operators are generator-independent, so \system\ materializes
each (query, rung) retrieval result once and shares it across every
generator family and every policy evaluated downstream. Certification
itself never re-executes plans: all candidates are evaluated against
the materialized execution matrix, which is what makes plan spaces of
$10^5$ candidates tractable (Section~\ref{sec:exp-scale}).

\subsection{Progressive knowledge-acquisition policies}
\label{sec:families}

From the rewrite lattice, \system\ materializes a \emph{cost-ascending
ladder} $P_0 \prec P_1 \prec \dots \prec P_{L-1}$ --- a maximal chain,
for example from sparse-only retrieval with a flash-tier model up to
hybrid retrieval with reranking, verification, and a max-tier model.
Each execution of rung $j$ on query $q$ emits a \emph{sufficiency
score} $s_j(q) \in [0,1]$: a calibrated probability that stopping here
satisfies the contract, predicted from runtime observables only ---
retrieval margins, cross-encoder scores, NLI support of the drafted
answer over its evidence, citation coverage, evidence ages, and
abstention markers. Scores are fit on the training split by per-rung
logistic models.

Three candidate families lift the ladder into policies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T-family (escalation)}: thresholds
$\lambda = (\lambda_0, \dots, \lambda_{L-2})$; execute rungs upward and
stop at the first $s_j(q) \ge \lambda_j$ (the final rung always
answers). Thresholds are parameterized along a one-dimensional quantile
path from most conservative to always-stop.
\item \textbf{U-family (routing)}: jump directly to the rung
maximizing $\hat{s}_j(q) - \lambda\, \bar{c}_j$ over $j$ --- a utility
router
sweeping $\lambda$ over a grid; it pays only the chosen rung's cost but
forfeits the escalation fallback.
\item \textbf{F-family (static)}: the $L$ fixed plans themselves.
\item \textbf{D-family (selective escalation)}: any of the above with
one more knob $\kappa$ --- if even the final rung's sufficiency score
falls below $\kappa$, the query is \emph{deferred} to human review
instead of answered. Deferral turns the contract into the pair
$\Prob(\text{answered} \wedge \text{violating}) \le \alpha$ and
$\Prob(\text{deferred}) \le \beta$; both losses are bounded, so the
same certification machinery applies unchanged. This family is what
makes strict contracts ($\alpha \le 0.2$) deployable on workloads
where every always-answer plan is infeasible
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-cost}).
\end{itemize}
Group-conditional variants (per query type or per dynamism class) apply
any family within each group. For a grouping $G(q) \in \{1..m\}$ known
at query time, \system\ certifies a threshold family per group on the
group's calibration subsample, each at level $\delta/m$, yielding the
stronger guarantee $R_j(\hat\pi \mid G = g) \le \alpha_j$ for all
groups simultaneously with probability at least $1-\delta$. The
candidate set $\mathcal{C} = \{\pi_1, \pi_2, \dots\}$ is finite but
large; every candidate is fully specified by training data alone.

The escalation family realizes, in statistical form, the database idea
of progressive re-optimization
\citep{kabra1998efficient,markl2004robust}: classical progressive
execution compares runtime cardinalities against optimizer estimates
and re-plans when they diverge; \system\ compares runtime sufficiency
evidence against certified thresholds and escalates to a stronger plan
when the current rung cannot demonstrate contract compliance. The
critical difference is that the escalation rule itself carries the
contract guarantee (Theorem~\ref{thm:validity}): the thresholds are not
tuned heuristics but the output of risk-controlled selection, so ``how
sure must rung $j$ be before answering'' is exactly as conservative as
the contract demands, no more.

\subsection{Certification by fixed-sequence testing}
\label{sec:certification}

For candidate $\pi$ and constraint $j$, the empirical risk on $n$
calibration queries is $\hat R_j(\pi)$. We use the Hoeffding--Bentkus
p-value \citep{bentkus2004hoeffding,angelopoulos2021ltt} for the null
hypothesis $H_0^{(j)}(\pi) : R_j(\pi) > \alpha_j$:
\[
p^{(j)}_{\pi} = \min\Big( e^{-n\,h_1(\hat R_j \wedge \alpha_j,\,
\alpha_j)},\
e\,\Prob\big(\mathrm{Bin}(n, \alpha_j) \le \lceil n \hat R_j \rceil
\big) \Big),
\]
with $h_1$ the Bernoulli Kullback--Leibler divergence. For
multi-constraint contracts the null is a union $\bigcup_j H_0^{(j)}$,
so by the intersection--union principle \citep{berger1982iut} the test
``reject iff $p^{(j)}_\pi \le \delta$ for every $j$'' has size at most
$\delta$ --- no Bonferroni splitting across constraints is needed.

Candidates are ordered $\pi_{(1)}, \pi_{(2)}, \dots$ by training-split
risk (safest first), ties broken by training cost --- an ordering fixed
before calibration data is seen. \system\ walks the sequence, certifies
each candidate whose composite test rejects, and stops at the first
failure; among certified candidates it deploys the cheapest by
calibration cost. Algorithm~\ref{alg:certify} summarizes the complete
deployment cycle, including the post-deployment monitoring loop of
Section~\ref{sec:monitor}. Two budget-accounting regimes must not be
conflated. A \emph{single-epoch} deployment --- certify once, no
monitoring loop --- may spend the whole budget on its one
certification; this is the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:validity} and
of the certification experiments in Section~\ref{sec:results}.
\emph{Lifetime} operation instead treats the initial certification as
epoch 0's certification: Algorithm~\ref{alg:certify} runs every
epoch's walk at level $\delta_k/2$ --- the initial one at
$\delta_0/2 = \delta/4$, not $\delta$ --- so certifications and
monitors over \emph{all} epochs sum to exactly
$\sum_{k \ge 0} \delta_k = \delta$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:lifetime}); no stage borrows error probability
from the future.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Certified lifetime deployment of a RAG knowledge service}
\label{alg:certify}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE contract $C = \{(\ell_j, \alpha_j)\}_{j \le d}$ with
\emph{lifetime} budget $\delta$; train split $Z_{\mathrm{tr}}$;
calibration split $Z_{1:n}$; plan lattice $\mathcal{P}$
\STATE annotate each rewrite edge of $\mathcal{P}$ with a risk voucher
$\hat\eps_r$ on $Z_{\mathrm{tr}}$; compose vouchers along chains by
telescoping (Proposition~\ref{prop:composition})
\STATE build candidate policies $\Pi$ (ladders, routers, deferral,
group-conditional) on $Z_{\mathrm{tr}}$; fix the walk order
$\pi_{(1)}, \pi_{(2)}, \dots$ by training risk, vouchers breaking ties
\FOR{epochs $k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ with budget
$\delta_k = \delta/2^{k+1}$}
  \STATE $Z^{(k)} \leftarrow$ epoch-$k$ calibration data ($Z_{1:n}$
  for $k = 0$; the fresh post-change window for $k \ge 1$)
  \STATE $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \emptyset$ \COMMENT{certified prefix}
  \FOR{$i = 1, 2, \dots, |\Pi|$}
    \STATE compute $p^{(j)}_{\pi_{(i)}}$ on $Z^{(k)}$ for every
    constraint $j$
    \IF{$\max_j p^{(j)}_{\pi_{(i)}} \le \delta_k/2$}
      \STATE $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \mathcal{S} \cup \{\pi_{(i)}\}$
    \ELSE
      \STATE \textbf{break} \COMMENT{first failure stops the walk}
    \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
  \IF{$\mathcal{S} = \emptyset$}
    \STATE report \emph{infeasible}; stay in flagged safe mode
    \COMMENT{never silently deployed}
  \ELSE
    \STATE deploy $\hat\pi_k \leftarrow \arg\min_{\pi \in \mathcal{S}}
    \hat c(\pi)$
  \ENDIF
  \STATE run a fresh horizon-$T$ monitor $M_t$ of
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixdetector} at level $\delta_k/2$ on audited
  violations
  \IF{$M_t \ge 2/\delta_k$ \textbf{or} horizon $T$ exhausted}
    \STATE enter safe mode; collect a fresh post-change calibration
    window; advance to epoch $k+1$
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Contract validity]
\label{thm:validity}
Let $\hat\pi$ be the policy returned by the fixed-sequence procedure.
Then $\Prob\big(R_j(\hat\pi) \le \alpha_j \ \forall j\big) \ge
1-\delta$, i.e., \system\ is $(C,\delta)$-certified, for any number of
candidates, any score quality, and any distribution $\mathcal{D}$.
\end{theorem}

The proof (\ref{app:proofs}) rests on the walk stopping at the first
non-rejection: an infeasible candidate can be certified only if the
walk reaches it and its size-$\delta$ test falsely rejects. Two
properties matter in practice. \emph{Selection-proofness}: the
guarantee is unaffected by how many candidates precede the stopping
point, so enriching $\Pi$ (new routers, new ladders) never costs
statistical validity. \emph{Power}: if the training-risk order is
aligned with the true order --- which we verify empirically --- the
walk penetrates deep into the cheap region before stopping.

Because the walk stops at its first failure, what the procedure
returns is, by construction, \emph{the cheapest policy of the
certified prefix} --- not necessarily the cheapest feasible policy of
the whole family. The following proposition states when the two
coincide and at what statistical price; when its ordering premise
fails, validity (Theorem~\ref{thm:validity}) is untouched and only
optimality degrades.

\begin{proposition}[Cost consistency]
\label{prop:consistency}
Let $M$ be the total number of candidates. Suppose along the walk
order the true risks are non-decreasing up to the cheapest feasible
candidate $\pi^\dagger$, which sits at position $K \le M$ with margin
$R(\pi^\dagger) \le \alpha - \gamma$, $\gamma > 0$, and that per-query
costs lie in $[0, C_{\max}]$. Then with probability at least
$1 - 2\delta'$ the procedure certifies every candidate up to and
including $\pi^\dagger$ once
$n \gtrsim \gamma^{-2}\big(\log(1/\delta) \vee \log(K/\delta')\big)$,
and the deployed policy $\hat\pi$ --- selected as cheapest by
\emph{estimated} calibration cost among \emph{all} certified
candidates, which may extend beyond position $K$ --- satisfies
\[
c(\hat\pi) \;\le\; c(\pi^\dagger)
+ 2\,C_{\max}\sqrt{\tfrac{\log(2M/\delta')}{2n}},
\]
where $c(\cdot)$ is the true mean cost: the uniform cost-estimation
error is controlled over the full candidate set --- since the certified
set is not known in advance --- and vanishes at rate
$O(\sqrt{\log M / n})$.
\end{proposition}

The $\log K$ and $\log M$ terms are the price of certifying the
$K$-prefix and estimating all $M$ costs simultaneously; both enter
only logarithmically, so enriching the candidate set is statistically
cheap. (Alternatively, restricting the cheapest-selection to the first
$K$ walk positions, or estimating costs on an independent split,
replaces $\log M$ by $\log K$ at no cost to validity.) Certification
cost is negligible computationally: it re-uses the ladder executions
already collected for calibration and needs no additional LLM calls
beyond the $n \times L$ calibration matrix.

\subsection{Anytime-valid drift monitoring and certified recalibration}
\label{sec:monitor}

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_runtime.pdf}
\caption{Certified progressive execution and the monitoring loop.
Escalation thresholds carry the contract guarantee; the e-process
monitor watches audited violations and, on alarm, drives safe-mode
execution, changepoint-aware recalibration, and redeployment.}
\label{fig:runtime}
\end{figure}

Certificates are only as good as the exchangeability between
calibration and deployment; deployed knowledge services drift ---
workload mix shifts, indexes fall behind the world, APIs degrade.
\system\ monitors deployed violation indicators $\ell_t \in [0,1]$ with
a \emph{restart-mixture} e-detector
\citep{shin2024edetectors,ramdas2023savi}
(Figure~\ref{fig:runtime}). Fix a horizon $T$ and a finite bet grid
$W \subset (0, 1/\alpha)$. For restart time $r$ and bet $w$, the wealth
process is
$E_t^{(r,w)} = \prod_{i=r}^{t} \big(1 + w\,(\ell_i - \alpha)\big)$
(with $E_t^{(r,w)} = 1$ for $t < r$). Rather than taking a maximum over
restarts --- which is not an e-process and cannot be scaled by Ville's
inequality --- the detector statistic is the weighted mixture
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mixdetector}
M_t \;=\; \sum_{r=1}^{t}\sum_{w \in W}
  \frac{1}{T\,|W|}\, E_t^{(r,w)} \;+\; \Big(1 - \frac{t}{T}\Big),
\end{equation}
where the trailing term is the prior mass of restarts not yet started.
The mixture weights sum to one, so $M_t$ is itself a nonnegative
supermartingale with $M_0 = 1$; an alarm fires when
$M_t \ge 1/\delta$. A simple recursion maintains all started restarts
of each bet jointly, so the update is $O(|W|) = O(1)$ per query in time
and space, independent of $t$.

\begin{proposition}[Anytime validity]
\label{prop:eprocess}
If the conditional risk stays within contract,
$\E[\ell_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \le \alpha$ for all $t$, then for
$M_t$ in~\eqref{eq:mixdetector},
$\Prob(\exists t \le T : M_t \ge 1/\delta) \le \delta$.
\end{proposition}

Seeding a fresh restart at every step is what makes the mixture
practical, and it recovers the ``forgetting'' that a single product
e-process lacks: a plain product sinks exponentially during a long
compliant prefix and goes numb, whereas in~\eqref{eq:mixdetector} a
change at any time $\tau$ is caught by the restart component born at
$\tau$, whose growth is unaffected by the compliant history before it.
The detection delay therefore depends on the horizon but not on
$\tau$.

\begin{proposition}[Detection delay]
\label{prop:delay}
Suppose a persistent change at $\tau$ makes
$\E[\ell_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \ge \alpha' > \alpha$ for all
$t > \tau$, and let
$g^\ast = \max_{w \in W}\, \E\big[\log(1 + w(\ell_t - \alpha))\big]$
be the best post-change log-growth rate on the grid ($g^\ast > 0$
whenever some $w \in W$ bets in the right direction, with
$g^\ast = \Theta(\mathrm{KL}(\alpha' \Vert \alpha))$ for a grid that
brackets the Kelly bet). Then for any $\delta' \in (0,1)$, with
probability at least $1 - \delta'$ the monitor alarms by time
$\tau + N$ with
\[
N \;=\; O\!\left(\frac{\log(1/\delta) + \log(T|W|) +
\log(1/\delta')}{g^\ast}\right),
\]
a bound independent of the change time $\tau$ and of the length of the
preceding compliant stretch.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Changepoint localization}
Alongside the mixture, \system\ maintains one $O(1)$ CUSUM-style
recursion: the log-wealth of a single restarted product that resets
whenever it falls to zero; at alarm time, the start of its
currently-growing run is the standard changepoint estimate $\hat\tau$.
Validity comes exclusively from the mixture $M_t$; the recursion is a
free diagnostic that tells the operator which prefix of audited losses
still reflects the old regime --- exactly the window recalibration must
exclude.

\paragraph{Closed-loop certified recalibration}
An alarm is a statistical statement (some prefix was out of contract),
not a repaired policy. On alarm, \system\ (i) switches to \emph{safe
mode} --- the strongest rung, optionally with deferral to human
review. Safe mode is a best-effort fallback, not a certified policy:
no plan can carry a certificate for a distribution that has not yet
been sampled, and we flag it as such. (ii) It collects a recalibration
window of audited post-alarm queries; (iii) re-runs the fixed-sequence
walk of Section~\ref{sec:certification} on that window with the
epoch's certification budget; and (iv) deploys the newly certified
policy --- or, if the new distribution is infeasible for every
candidate, reports non-certifiability and stays in flagged safe mode.
The recalibration certificate is the \emph{only} post-drift guarantee,
and it is with respect to the post-change distribution, requiring only
that the post-alarm window be exchangeable with what follows --- drift
before the alarm is irrelevant by construction. Between alarms no
labels are wasted: the same audited losses double as the next
calibration set.

\paragraph{Lifetime validity across repeated alarms}

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_lifetime.png}
\caption{Lifetime operation as a sequence of finite monitoring epochs.
An epoch ends either at an alarm (red star) or when its monitor
exhausts the horizon $T$ (blue circle, scheduled restart); both events
trigger recertification under the next geometric budget
$\delta_k = \delta/2^{k+1}$, so validity holds simultaneously over
arbitrarily many epochs (Proposition~\ref{prop:lifetime}).}
\label{fig:lifetime}
\end{figure}

A deployment does not stop after one alarm, and re-using a fixed
$\delta/2$ per epoch would let error probabilities accumulate without
bound; moreover, each monitor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixdetector} is defined
for a \emph{finite} horizon $T$, so unbounded operation must be built
from finite blocks (Figure~\ref{fig:lifetime}). \system\ does both with one mechanism: the
lifetime is divided into \emph{epochs}, where an epoch ends either at
an alarm or when its monitor exhausts its horizon $T$ without alarming
--- a \emph{scheduled restart} that triggers the same recertification
path as an alarm and re-uses the epoch's audited losses. Epoch
$k \ge 0$ receives budget
$\delta_k = \delta/2^{k+1}$, split evenly between its certification
($\delta_k/2$) and its fresh horizon-$T$ monitor ($\delta_k/2$); the
\emph{initial} certification is epoch 0's certification and therefore
runs at level $\delta_0/2 = \delta/4$, not at the full $\delta$
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:certify}). Since
$\sum_{k \ge 0} \delta_k = \delta$, the union bound over every
certification and every monitor holds over an
\emph{unbounded} horizon while every monitor operates strictly inside
the finite-$T$ regime where
Propositions~\ref{prop:eprocess}--\ref{prop:delay} apply.

\begin{proposition}[Lifetime validity]
\label{prop:lifetime}
Run the alarm--recalibrate loop for arbitrarily many epochs
(alarm-triggered or horizon-triggered), giving
epoch $k$'s certification budget $\delta_k/2$ and its monitor budget
$\delta_k/2$ with $\delta_k = \delta/2^{k+1}$, the initial
certification being epoch 0's (level $\delta/4$). Then with probability
at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously over the whole deployment
lifetime: every epoch's certified policy satisfies its contract on
that epoch's distribution, and no compliant epoch raises a false
alarm. By Proposition~\ref{prop:delay}, the $k$-th monitor's detection
delay bound grows only additively, by $(k+2)\log 2 \,/\, g^\ast$
relative to a fixed budget $\delta$.
\end{proposition}

The geometric schedule prices repeated adaptation honestly: each
additional epoch costs one halving of the remaining budget, which
enters delays and certificate widths logarithmically rather than
exhausting them (proof in \ref{app:proofs}). An alternative is an
infinite-horizon restart prior (weights $\propto 1/(r(r+1))$), which
removes $T$ entirely but makes the delay bound inherit a $\log\tau$
dependence on the change time; the block construction preserves
change-time independence, which is why we adopt it.

\paragraph{Monitoring from subsampled audits}
The monitor consumes audited violation indicators, and auditing costs
money (an LLM judge call, an NLI entailment check, or a human label).
Full-traffic auditing is not required: if each deployed query is
audited independently with probability $r$, the audited subsequence is
itself an i.i.d.\ draw from the deployment distribution, so every
guarantee in this section applies verbatim to the audited stream ---
only the clock dilates, multiplying detection delays by $1/r$ in
wall-clock queries. The audit rate is thus an explicit dial between
monitoring cost and reaction time, and audit spend is part of the
system's cost of ownership, which we meter and report in
Section~\ref{sec:exp-drift}.
