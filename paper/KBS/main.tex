\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds large language models
(LLMs) in external knowledge sources, yet the reliability of the
resulting knowledge service remains uncalibrated: practitioners tune
pipelines on development sets and hope the observed quality persists in
deployment. Existing adaptive RAG systems reduce cost with routing or
cascading heuristics, but none can state, with quantified confidence,
what fraction of deployed answers will violate an application's quality,
citation, freshness, or latency requirements. We propose \emph{evidence
contracts}, a declarative specification of admissible violation rates
for a RAG knowledge service, and \system, a framework that certifies
contract feasibility by statistical testing rather than point-estimate
comparison. \system\ organizes cost-decreasing pipeline rewrites into a
plan lattice annotated with assumption-free \emph{risk vouchers},
materializes progressive knowledge-acquisition policies --- including
policies that defer to human review under strict contracts --- over
the lattice, and deploys the cheapest policy in the prefix certified
by a distribution-free fixed-sequence test on calibration data,
yielding a finite-sample guarantee that all contract constraints hold
with probability at least $1-\delta$. After deployment, an
anytime-valid e-process monitor --- valid also under subsampled
auditing --- detects knowledge drift with a change-time-independent
delay bound, localizes the changepoint, and drives a recalibration
loop whose geometric error-budget schedule preserves validity over
arbitrarily many alarms. Experiments on four heterogeneous benchmarks
(HybridQA, CRAG, ASQA, QAMPARI) with fully metered execution verify
the guarantee directly on exact finite-population risk: across 1000
repeated calibration draws, \system's deployed policies exceed their
contract in at most 0.8\% of draws against a 10\% budget, versus up to
64\% for Bayesian optimization and empirical tuning, while cutting LLM
cost by up to 20$\times$ against the strongest fixed pipeline
(10--18$\times$ after charging monitoring audits). The guarantee
transfers unchanged across four LLM families from four model
developers and two serving backends, including a locally served
open-weight family metered in GPU-seconds. These results suggest that
knowledge-intensive LLM systems should move beyond point-estimate
tuning toward certified, contract-driven deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper reframed the deployment of retrieval-augmented generation as
a certified knowledge-service problem. Evidence contracts give
applications a declarative vocabulary for reliability --- admissible
violation rates for answer quality, citation completeness, evidence
freshness, and latency --- and \system\ makes the vocabulary
enforceable: plan rewrites are priced by assumption-free risk vouchers,
progressive knowledge-acquisition policies are searched over a plan
lattice, feasibility is established by a distribution-free
fixed-sequence test whose guarantee survives unlimited search, and an
anytime-valid e-process monitor defends the certificate against
knowledge drift with certified recalibration, geometric error spending
across repeated alarms, and explicitly metered audit costs. Across
four heterogeneous benchmarks and four LLM families from four model
developers, certification was the only mechanism that kept the
declared promise --- verified on exact finite-population risk, at most
0.8\% violation probability against a 10\% budget, where uncertified
tuning violated in up to 64\% of draws --- while remaining up to
20$\times$ cheaper than the strongest fixed pipeline before audit
costs and 10--18$\times$ after. We believe contract-driven
certification offers a principled and practical path for
knowledge-based systems that must be simultaneously economical and
accountable, and we release all execution matrices and code to support
replication and extension.
