\title{\system: Certified Contract-Aware Query Optimization\\for Hybrid Retrieval-Augmented Generation}

\author{Anonymous}
\affiliation{\institution{Anonymous}\country{}}

\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines over heterogeneous data ---
tables, text corpora, vector indexes, and knowledge-graph APIs --- expose
dozens of execution knobs whose settings induce order-of-magnitude cost
differences at similar answer quality. Existing systems either fix one
pipeline for an entire workload, or route queries with uncalibrated
heuristics; neither can promise anything about the fraction of answers that
will violate an application's quality, citation, freshness, or latency
requirements. We introduce \emph{evidence contracts}, a declarative interface
in which the application states admissible violation rates, and \system, a
query optimizer that treats contract feasibility as a statistical testing
problem rather than a point-estimate comparison. \system searches a
heterogeneous policy space --- escalation ladders derived from cost-reducing
plan rewrites, model-selection routers, group-conditional variants, and,
under strict contracts, policies with certified deferral to human review
--- and deploys the cheapest policy in the prefix certified by a
distribution-free fixed-sequence test on calibration data, yielding
finite-sample contract guarantees. Approximate rewrites carry empirical
\emph{risk vouchers} that
guide plan enumeration; an anytime-valid e-process monitor --- valid also
under subsampled auditing --- detects workload
and data drift after deployment and drives a recalibration loop whose
geometric error-budget schedule stays valid over arbitrarily many
alarms. On four hybrid
RAG benchmarks (HybridQA, CRAG, ASQA, QAMPARI) with real token-metered
execution, we verify the guarantee directly on exact finite-population
risk: across 1000 calibration draws \system's deployed policies exceed
their contract in at most 0.8\% of draws against a $\delta=10\%$ budget,
while empirical tuning, Bayesian
optimization, and router baselines violate in up to 64\% of draws; at
matched contract levels \system cuts LLM cost against the strongest fixed
pipeline by up to 20$\times$ (10--18$\times$ once monitoring audits are
metered), and the guarantee carries unchanged across four generator
families from four model developers on two serving backends ---
including a locally served open-weight family metered in GPU-seconds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
RAG turned query optimization into a statistical discipline: plans are
inequivalent, quality is stochastic, and constraints can only be
established by inference. \system\ draws the consequence --- feasibility
by hypothesis test; search guided but never validated by learned
estimates (selectivity-driven access paths, telescoping risk vouchers);
and monitoring by an anytime-valid restart-mixture e-detector that
localizes drift, tolerates subsampled audits, and closes the loop with
certified recalibration under a lifetime error budget ---
and shows on four hybrid benchmarks and four generator families from
four model developers that this discipline costs little and buys the
only thing an application can build on: a promise that holds. We see
evidence contracts as a natural interface for the emerging semantic data
stack, and certified optimization as the execution-engine counterpart that
makes the interface meaningful.

\begin{acks}
Datasets: HybridQA/WikiTables, CRAG (+ official mock KG API), ALCE-ASQA,
ALCE-QAMPARI. Evaluation follows each benchmark's official protocol; LLM
executions are token-metered DashScope calls (Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM) and
GPU-second-metered local Ollama serving (Gemma~3).
\end{acks}

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Theorem~\ref{thm:validity}}
Fix the candidate order $\pi_{(1)}, \pi_{(2)}, \dots$ (a function of the
train split only, hence independent of the calibration sample). For
candidate $\pi$ let $H_0(\pi) = \bigcup_j \{R_j(\pi) > \alpha_j\}$ be its
composite null. The per-candidate test rejects iff every constraint's
Hoeffding--Bentkus p-value is $\le \delta$. Each $p^{(j)}_\pi$ is a valid
p-value for $H_0^{(j)}$ \cite{bentkus2004hoeffding,angelopoulos2021ltt};
by the intersection--union principle \cite{berger1982iut}, when
$H_0(\pi)$ holds at least one $H_0^{(j)}$ holds, so
$\Prob(\text{reject } \pi) \le \Prob(p^{(j^\ast)}_\pi \le \delta) \le
\delta$ for a true null $j^\ast$: the composite test has size $\le\delta$.
Let $k^\ast$ be the index of the first infeasible candidate in the order
(if none, no error is possible). The procedure deploys an infeasible
candidate only if the walk certifies $\pi_{(k^\ast)}$ (it cannot reach
later candidates without doing so, and all earlier candidates are
feasible). That event has probability $\le \delta$. Choosing the cheapest
\emph{certified} candidate only ever selects among candidates that were
certified, so the error event is unchanged. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:composition}}
Write $\rho_i^{\mathrm{step}} = \Prob_q(\ell(P_i, q) > \ell(P_{i-1}, q))$
for the stepwise harm of $R_i$ against its predecessor. \emph{Deterministic
inclusion}: for any query $q$ with $\ell(P_m, q) > \ell(P_0, q)$, the
telescoping sum $\sum_{i=1}^m [\ell(P_i, q) - \ell(P_{i-1}, q)] =
\ell(P_m, q) - \ell(P_0, q) > 0$ forces at least one strictly positive
summand, i.e., $q \in \{\ell(P_i) > \ell(P_{i-1})\}$ for some $i$. Hence
$\{\ell(P_m) > \ell(P_0)\} \subseteq \bigcup_i \{\ell(P_i) >
\ell(P_{i-1})\}$ pointwise, with no assumption on the rewrites. Taking
probabilities, $\rho(R_m \cdots R_1; P_0) \le \sum_i \rho_i^{\mathrm{step}}$.
Each step voucher satisfies $\Prob(\rho_i^{\mathrm{step}} >
\hat\eps_i^{\mathrm{step}}) \le \delta_r$ (Clopper--Pearson); on the event
that all $m$ bounds hold (probability $\ge 1 - m\delta_r$ by a union
bound over the $m$ vouchers, the only stochastic step),
$\rho(R_m \cdots R_1; P_0) \le \sum_i \hat\eps_i^{\mathrm{step}}$.
\hfill$\square$

\emph{Remark.} The base-relative union bound (each rewrite vouchered
against $P_0$) requires the additional no-synergistic-harm assumption
$\{\ell(P_m) > \ell(P_0)\} \subseteq \bigcup_i \{\ell(R_i(P_0)) >
\ell(P_0)\}$, which composed rewrites violate on real workloads
(\S\ref{sec:exp-rewrites}); the telescoping decomposition replaces that
behavioral assumption with an algebraic identity by conditioning each
voucher on the actual chain prefix.

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:eprocess}}
Under the null $\E[\ell_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \le \alpha$, each per-step
factor satisfies
$\E[1 + w(\ell_t - \alpha) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]
= 1 + w(\E[\ell_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] - \alpha) \le 1$
for any $w \in (0, 1/\alpha)$ and $\ell_t \in [0,1]$ (the factor is also
nonnegative since $w < 1/\alpha$ and $\ell_t \ge 0$). Hence each restarted
product $E_t^{(r,w)} = \prod_{i=r}^{t}(1 + w(\ell_i - \alpha))$ is a
nonnegative supermartingale, equal to $1$ for $t < r$, so
$\E[E_t^{(r,w)} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \le E_{t-1}^{(r,w)}$ for all $t$.
The statistic in~\eqref{eq:mixdetector},
\[
M_t = \sum_{r=1}^{t}\sum_{w \in W}\frac{1}{T|W|}\,E_t^{(r,w)}
      + \Big(1 - \frac{t}{T}\Big),
\]
is a fixed convex combination of these supermartingales and the constants
$1$ (the not-yet-started restarts $r > t$, each contributing
$\tfrac{1}{T|W|}\cdot 1$, which total $1 - t/T$). A convex combination of
nonnegative supermartingales, with weights fixed in advance and summing to
one, is again a nonnegative supermartingale, and $M_0 = 1$. This is exactly
where the mixture is needed: a \emph{maximum} $\max_{r} E_t^{(r,w)}$ is not
a supermartingale (the maximizing index is chosen with hindsight), so Ville
would not apply to it. Applying Ville's inequality \cite{ville1939etude} to
$M_t$ gives $\Prob(\exists t \le T : M_t \ge 1/\delta)
= \Prob(\sup_{t \le T} M_t \ge 1/\delta) \le \E[M_0]\,\delta = \delta$.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:delay}}
Consider the single mixture component with restart $r = \tau + 1$ and the
growth-optimal grid bet $w^\ast \in \arg\max_{w \in W}\,
\E[\log(1 + w(\ell_t - \alpha))]$, whose post-change per-step log-growth is
$g^\ast > 0$. Its increments
$X_i = \log\big(1 + w^\ast(\ell_i - \alpha)\big)$ are bounded
($|X_i| \le \log\frac{1}{1-w^\ast\alpha} \vee \log(1 + w^\ast(1-\alpha))
=: b$) with conditional mean $\ge g^\ast$ for $i > \tau$. By the
Azuma--Hoeffding bound applied to $\sum_{i=\tau+1}^{\tau+n}(X_i - g^\ast)$,
with probability $\ge 1 - \delta'$,
\[
\log E_{\tau+n}^{(\tau+1, w^\ast)}
= \sum_{i=\tau+1}^{\tau+n} X_i
\ \ge\ n g^\ast - b\sqrt{2 n \log(1/\delta')}.
\]
Since $M_{\tau+n} \ge \frac{1}{T|W|}E_{\tau+n}^{(\tau+1,w^\ast)}$, the alarm
threshold $M_{\tau+n} \ge 1/\delta$ is reached once
$n g^\ast - b\sqrt{2 n \log(1/\delta')} \ge \log(1/\delta) + \log(T|W|)$.
Solving this scalar inequality gives
$n = O\big((\log(1/\delta) + \log(T|W|) + \log(1/\delta'))/g^\ast\big)$,
independent of $\tau$. For a Bernoulli loss the Kelly bet
$w^\ast = \frac{\alpha'-\alpha}{\alpha'(1-\alpha)}$ attains
$g^\ast = \mathrm{KL}(\alpha' \Vert \alpha)$; any grid $W$ bracketing it
loses only a constant factor, giving the stated
$\mathrm{KL}(\alpha'\Vert\alpha)$ rate. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:consistency}}
Let $\pi^\dagger$ be the cheapest candidate with $R(\pi^\dagger) \le
\alpha - \gamma$, at position $K$ in the walk, with all true risks
non-decreasing along the order up to $K$.
\emph{Certification of the prefix.} For every $\pi_{(k)}$, $k \le
K$: $R(\pi_{(k)}) \le \alpha - \gamma$, and by Hoeffding the empirical
risk is $\le \alpha - \gamma/2$ with probability
$1 - e^{-n\gamma^2/2}$; the HB p-value at empirical risk
$\alpha - \gamma/2$ is $\le e^{-n\,h_1(\alpha-\gamma/2,\alpha)} \le
e^{-n\gamma^2/2}$, which is $\le \delta$ once
$n \ge 2\gamma^{-2}\log(1/\delta)$. A union bound over the $K$ candidates
(at level $\delta'/K$ each) shows all are certified simultaneously with
probability $\ge 1 - \delta'$ when
$n \ge 2\gamma^{-2}(\log(1/\delta) \vee \log(K/\delta'))$; on this event
the walk reaches at least position $K$ and $\pi^\dagger$ is certified.
\emph{Uniform cost estimation.} The walk does not stop at $K$ --- it
continues until the first failed test --- so the deployed policy may
sit at any position in the certified set, whose extent is
data-dependent and not known in advance. We therefore control cost
estimates uniformly over \emph{all} $M$ candidates: per-query costs
lie in $[0, C_{\max}]$,
so by Hoeffding plus a union bound over the $M$ candidates (at level
$\delta'/M$ each), with
probability $\ge 1 - \delta'$ every candidate's estimated mean
calibration cost satisfies $|\hat c(\pi) - c(\pi)| \le \eps_n :=
C_{\max}\sqrt{\log(2M/\delta')/(2n)}$ simultaneously --- in particular
for $\hat\pi$ and $\pi^\dagger$, wherever the walk terminates. The
deployed
$\hat\pi$ minimizes $\hat c$ over the certified set, which contains
$\pi^\dagger$, hence $c(\hat\pi) \le \hat c(\hat\pi) + \eps_n \le
\hat c(\pi^\dagger) + \eps_n \le c(\pi^\dagger) + 2\eps_n$. Both events
hold together with probability $\ge 1 - 2\delta'$. (Restricting the
cheapest-selection to the first $K$ walk positions, or estimating
costs on an independent split, shrinks the union bound from $M$ to $K$
at no cost to validity.) Without the
monotone-order premise the walk may stop before $K$; the
certified-prefix optimality and the $2\eps_n$ bound continue to hold
with $\pi^\dagger$ replaced by the cheapest feasible candidate of the
certified prefix, and validity is unaffected.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proposition~\ref{prop:lifetime}}
Index epochs $k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ by their terminating events ---
an alarm or the exhaustion of the monitor's horizon $T$ (scheduled
restart). Each epoch lasts at most $T$ queries, so an unbounded
lifetime is covered by countably many epochs, and each epoch's monitor
is a fresh finite-horizon detector run strictly within its own block
--- the regime in which Proposition~\ref{prop:eprocess} holds. Epoch
$k$'s certification is a fixed-sequence test at level $\delta_k/2$ on
data from that epoch alone --- the initial certification is epoch 0's,
run at $\delta_0/2 = \delta/4$ on the initial calibration split, so it
sits inside the schedule rather than adding a $\delta$-sized event on
top of it --- and by Theorem~\ref{thm:validity} the
probability that epoch $k$ deploys a policy violating its contract (on
the epoch's distribution) is at most $\delta_k/2$; epoch $k$'s monitor
is an e-detector at level $\delta_k/2$, so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:eprocess} its false-alarm probability while the
epoch is compliant is at most $\delta_k/2$. Horizon-triggered epoch
boundaries consume no additional error probability: exhausting $T$
without alarm is a deterministic event, and the subsequent
recertification is already accounted at level $\delta_{k+1}/2$. The
lifetime error event is
the union over $k$; by countable subadditivity its probability is at
most $\sum_{k \ge 0} \delta_k = \sum_{k \ge 0} \delta/2^{k+1} =
\delta$, with no bound on the number of epochs. For the delay claim,
the $k$-th monitor's threshold is $\log(2^{k+2}/\delta) =
\log(1/\delta) + (k+2)\log 2$, which enters
Proposition~\ref{prop:delay} additively, giving the stated
$(k+2)\log 2 / g^\ast$ increment; a change straddling a block boundary
is caught by the next block's monitor, adding at most the residual of
the current block to the delay. Under the infinite-horizon alternative
--- prior weights $\frac{1}{r(r+1)}$ over restart times $r$, which sum
to one --- validity holds with no horizon at all, but the mixture mass
at restart $\tau$ is $\Theta(\tau^{-2})$, so the threshold term
becomes $\log(\tau^2|W|)$ and the delay inherits a $\log\tau$
dependence on the change time; the block construction avoids this.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Subsampled audits}
Let each query be audited independently with probability $r$ (the coin
independent of the query) and run the monitor on the audited
subsequence. Conditional on being audited, a query is an unbiased draw
from the deployment distribution, so the audited losses satisfy the
same conditional-mean hypotheses as the full stream and
Propositions~\ref{prop:eprocess}--\ref{prop:lifetime} apply verbatim
with $t$ counting audited queries. A post-change window of $N$ audited
queries occupies $N/r$ wall-clock queries in expectation (concentrating
at rate $O(\sqrt{N}/r)$), giving the $1/r$ delay dilation of
\S\ref{sec:monitor}. \hfill$\square$
