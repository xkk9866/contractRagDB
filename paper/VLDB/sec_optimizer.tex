\section{Certified Plan Optimization}
\label{sec:optimizer}

\input{fig_arch}

Section~\ref{sec:contracts} reduced contract-aware deployment to the
constrained problem~\eqref{eq:problem}; this section gives the optimizer
that solves it with a certificate.
\system's optimizer (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}) has three layers: (i) a
\emph{rewrite layer} that generates a lattice of cost-decreasing plan
variants, each annotated with an empirical \emph{risk voucher}; (ii) a
\emph{policy layer} that lifts static plans into per-query adaptive
policies (escalation ladders, routers, group-conditional variants); and
(iii) a \emph{certification layer} that walks the candidate policies in a
data-independent order and outputs the cheapest one that passes a
distribution-free risk test. Only layer (iii) touches calibration data, and
it does so exactly once; layers (i)--(ii) use a disjoint training split.
Vouchers guide search order --- like a cost model, they may be wrong without
compromising correctness, only optimality.

\subsection{Cost-decreasing rewrites and risk vouchers}
\label{sec:rewrites}

\input{fig_rewrites}

A rewrite rule $R : P \mapsto P'$ transforms a plan into a cheaper one
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rewrites}):
retrieval truncation ($k \to k' < k$), reranker elision, source pre-filter
pushdown (restricting a passage pool to rows selected by a structured
predicate), generator downgrade ($g \to g'$ with a cheaper model), and
repair elision. Classical optimizers require $P \equiv P'$; here
equivalence fails and we quantify the damage.

\begin{definition}[Risk voucher]
\label{def:voucher}
For loss $\ell$ and rewrite $R$, let
$\rho(R; P) = \Prob_{q}\big(\ell(R(P), q) > \ell(P, q)\big)$ be the
\emph{harm probability}. An \emph{$(\hat\eps, \delta_r)$-voucher} for $R$ at
$P$ is a Clopper--Pearson upper bound $\hat\eps$ on $\rho(R;P)$ at
confidence $1-\delta_r$, computed from training executions of $P$ and
$R(P)$ on the same queries.
\end{definition}

\textbf{Assumption-free composition along paths.} The naive way to compose
vouchers measures each rewrite \emph{against the base plan} and sums the
bounds; that requires a no-synergistic-harm (NSH) assumption --- that the
chain harms no query unless some individual rewrite already does --- which
real workloads violate (\S\ref{sec:exp-rewrites}). \system\ instead
vouchers the \emph{path}: for the chain
$P_0 \xrightarrow{R_1} P_1 \xrightarrow{R_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{R_m} P_m$,
each step voucher $\hat\eps_i^{\mathrm{step}}$ bounds the harm of $R_i$
against its \emph{actual predecessor} $P_{i-1}$, not against $P_0$.

\begin{proposition}[Telescoping path composition]
\label{prop:composition}
If $\ell(P_m, q) > \ell(P_0, q)$ then some consecutive step strictly
increases the loss, so deterministically
$\{q : \ell(P_m,q) > \ell(P_0,q)\} \subseteq
 \bigcup_{i} \{q : \ell(P_i,q) > \ell(P_{i-1},q)\}$.
Hence if each step carries an $(\hat\eps_i^{\mathrm{step}},
\delta_r)$-voucher, then with probability $\ge 1 - m\delta_r$,
$\rho(R_m \cdots R_1; P_0) \le \sum_{i=1}^m \hat\eps_i^{\mathrm{step}}$
--- with \emph{no} behavioral assumption on rewrite interactions.
\end{proposition}

The inclusion is a tautology of the telescoping decomposition
$\ell(P_m) - \ell(P_0) = \sum_i [\ell(P_i) - \ell(P_{i-1})]$; the only
stochastic step is the $m$ Clopper--Pearson bounds. The price is that step
vouchers are chain-specific (they condition on the prefix), which matches
how the optimizer explores anyway: it descends one path of the rewrite
lattice at a time. \textbf{Selectivity-conditioned vouchers} refine this
further: harm of a pre-filter pushdown concentrates on queries whose
structured predicate is \emph{unselective} (the restricted pool drops
relevant evidence), so \system\ stratifies calibration queries by estimated
pool selectivity $\sigma$ (\S\ref{sec:planner}) and issues a per-stratum
Clopper--Pearson bound (Bonferroni across strata). The result is a
voucher that is tight where the rewrite is safe and honest where it is
not, the risk analogue of a selectivity-indexed cost model. Even so,
\system\ never uses vouchers as deployment guarantees: they order the
search space, while soundness comes exclusively from direct certification
of the deployed policy below. The division of labor mirrors classical
optimizers --- cardinality estimates pick the plan, the executor's
correctness does not depend on them.

\subsection{Cardinality-driven physical planning}
\label{sec:planner}

Hybrid RAG queries \emph{join} a structured source with an unstructured
one: on HybridQA-style workloads the evidence must connect table rows $S$
(selected by a structured predicate) with linked passages $T$ (a
row$\to$passage foreign key). Two join orders exist, and they are the
retrieval analogue of index-scan versus full-scan:
\emph{table-first} pushes the predicate down --- select rows, then
retrieve only from the selected rows' passage pool (small pool, cheap,
but drops evidence linked from unselected rows); \emph{text-first}
retrieves over the whole table's pool and joins back to rows (large pool,
higher recall on vague predicates). Which order wins is decided by the
predicate's \emph{selectivity}. \system\ estimates cardinalities before
retrieval: the number of rows matching the predicate, and the pushdown
and full pool sizes from the link table, giving a pool selectivity
$\sigma = |T_{\mathrm{push}}| / |T_{\mathrm{full}}|$ per query. A cost
model fit by least squares on measured executions (embedding cost linear
in pool size, rerank cost linear in candidates) and a recall model
$r(\sigma)$ fit on the training split (table-first recall degrades as
$\sigma \to 0$) drive a classical access-path rule: \emph{cheapest path
whose estimated recall clears the target; otherwise the highest-recall
path}. The chosen access path parameterizes the physical plans that the
rewrite and policy layers operate on; as with vouchers, cardinality
estimates affect only cost, never the certificate. The same estimates
index the selectivity-conditioned vouchers of \S\ref{sec:rewrites}, so
the planner and the risk layer share one selectivity abstraction.

\textbf{Operator sharing and materialization.} Retrieval operators are
generator-independent, so \system\ materializes each (query, rung)
retrieval result once and shares it across every generator family and
every policy evaluated downstream --- the ladder matrix is a shared
subplan in the sense of multi-query optimization. Certification itself
never re-executes plans: all $|\Pi|$ candidates are evaluated against the
materialized matrix, which is what makes plan spaces of $10^5$ candidates
tractable (\S\ref{sec:exp-system}).

\subsection{Policy families}
\label{sec:families}

From the rewrite lattice \system\ materializes a \emph{cost-ascending
ladder} $P_0 \prec P_1 \prec \dots \prec P_{L-1}$ (a maximal chain, e.g.,
sparse-only retrieval with a flash-tier model up to hybrid retrieval with
reranking, verification, and a max-tier model). Each execution of rung $j$
on query $q$ emits a \emph{sufficiency score} $s_j(q) \in [0,1]$ ---
a calibrated probability that stopping here satisfies the contract,
predicted from runtime observables only (retrieval margins, cross-encoder
scores, NLI support of the drafted answer over its evidence, citation
coverage, evidence ages, abstention markers). Scores are fit on the
training split by per-rung logistic models.

Three candidate families lift the ladder into policies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T-family (escalation)}: thresholds
$\lambda = (\lambda_0, \dots, \lambda_{L-2})$; execute rungs upward, stop at
the first $s_j(q) \ge \lambda_j$ (the final rung always answers). We
parameterize $\lambda$ along a one-dimensional quantile path
$q \mapsto \lambda(q)$, from most conservative to always-stop.
\item \textbf{U-family (routing)}: jump directly to rung
$\arg\max_j \hat{s}_j(q) - \lambda\, \bar{c}_j$ --- a utility router
sweeping $\lambda$ over a grid; pays only the chosen rung's cost but
forfeits the escalation fallback.
\item \textbf{F-family (static)}: the $L$ fixed plans themselves.
\end{itemize}
Group-conditional variants (per query-type or per dynamism class) apply any
family within each group (\S\ref{sec:runtime}). The candidate set
$\mathcal{C} = \{\pi_1, \pi_2, \dots\}$ is finite but large; every candidate
is fully specified by training data alone.

\subsection{Certification by fixed-sequence testing}
\label{sec:certification}

For candidate $\pi$ and constraint $j$, the empirical risk on $n$
calibration queries is $\hat R_j(\pi)$. We use the Hoeffding--Bentkus
p-value~\cite{bentkus2004hoeffding,angelopoulos2021ltt} for the null
$H_0^{(j)}(\pi) : R_j(\pi) > \alpha_j$:
\[
p^{(j)}_{\pi} = \min\Big( e^{-n\,h_1(\hat R_j \wedge \alpha_j,\, \alpha_j)},\,
e\,\Prob\big(\mathrm{Bin}(n, \alpha_j) \le \lceil n \hat R_j \rceil\big) \Big),
\]
with $h_1$ the Bernoulli KL divergence. For multi-constraint contracts the
null is a \emph{union} $\bigcup_j H_0^{(j)}$, so by the intersection--union
principle~\cite{berger1982iut} the test ``reject iff $p^{(j)}_\pi \le \delta$
for \emph{every} $j$'' has size $\le \delta$ --- no Bonferroni splitting
across constraints is needed.

Candidates are ordered $\pi_{(1)}, \pi_{(2)}, \dots$ by training-split risk
(safest first), ties broken by training cost --- an ordering fixed before
calibration data is seen. \system\ walks the sequence, certifies each
candidate whose (composite) test rejects, and \emph{stops at the first
failure}; among certified candidates it deploys the cheapest by calibration
cost:

\begin{theorem}[Contract validity]
\label{thm:validity}
Let $\hat\pi$ be the policy returned by the fixed-sequence procedure. Then
$\Prob\big(R_j(\hat\pi) \le \alpha_j \ \forall j\big) \ge 1-\delta$,
i.e., \system\ is $(C,\delta)$-certified, for any number of candidates,
any score quality, and any distribution $\mathcal{D}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Walking stops at the first non-rejection, so an infeasible candidate can be
certified only if the walk reaches it and its composite test falsely
rejects. Let $\pi_{(k^*)}$ be the first infeasible candidate in the order.
Every certified candidate before $k^*$ is feasible; the procedure errs only
if the size-$\delta$ IUT at $k^*$ falsely rejects, an event of probability
at most $\delta$. Deploying the cheapest certified candidate cannot
introduce additional error. \end{proof}

Two properties matter in practice. \emph{Selection-proofness}: the
guarantee is unaffected by how many candidates precede the stopping point,
so enriching $\Pi$ (new routers, new ladders) never costs statistical
validity. \emph{Power}: if the training-risk order is aligned with the true
order --- which we verify empirically --- the walk penetrates deep into the
cheap region before stopping. Because the walk stops at its first
failure, the returned policy is by construction \emph{the cheapest of
the certified prefix}, not necessarily the cheapest feasible policy of
the whole space; the next proposition states when the two coincide and
at what statistical price --- when its ordering premise fails, only
optimality degrades, never validity.

\begin{proposition}[Cost consistency]
\label{prop:consistency}
Let $M$ be the total number of candidates. Suppose along the walk order
the true risks are non-decreasing up to the
cheapest feasible candidate $\pi^\dagger$, which sits at position
$K \le M$
with margin $R(\pi^\dagger) \le \alpha - \gamma$, $\gamma > 0$, and that
per-query costs lie in $[0, C_{\max}]$. Then with probability
$\ge 1 - 2\delta'$ the procedure certifies every candidate up to and
including $\pi^\dagger$ once
\[
n \;\gtrsim\; \gamma^{-2}\big(\log(1/\delta) \vee \log(K/\delta')\big),
\]
and the deployed policy $\hat\pi$ --- cheapest by \emph{estimated}
calibration cost among \emph{all} certified candidates, which may
extend beyond position $K$ --- satisfies
$c(\hat\pi) \le c(\pi^\dagger)
+ 2 C_{\max}\sqrt{\log(2M/\delta')/(2n)}$
in true mean cost: since the certified set is not known in advance,
the cost-estimation error is controlled uniformly over the full
candidate set and vanishes at rate
$O(\sqrt{\log M / n})$. The $\log K$ and $\log M$ terms ---
simultaneous certification and simultaneous cost estimation --- enter
only logarithmically, so enriching the candidate set is statistically
cheap; restricting the cheapest-selection to the first $K$ positions,
or estimating costs on an independent split, replaces $\log M$ by
$\log K$.
\end{proposition}

Certification cost is negligible: it re-uses the ladder executions already
collected for calibration --- \system\ needs \emph{no} additional LLM calls
beyond the $n \times L$ calibration matrix, in contrast to per-deployment
MAB sampling~\cite{russo2025abacus}.
